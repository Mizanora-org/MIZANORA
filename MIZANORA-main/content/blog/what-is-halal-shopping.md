Many people hear "halal" and think only of food. In Islam, however, halal and haram apply to every part of life, including how we buy and sell. Halal shopping means that the product you buy is permissible, and that the way it is bought and sold is honest and fair. This guide explains the idea in simple language, for buyers and for sellers.

## Halal shopping has two parts

### 1. The product must be permissible

The item itself should not be something Islam forbids. Examples of clearly forbidden things include alcohol, pork, intoxicants and anything made for a haram purpose. Most everyday products, such as clothes, shoes, bags, home items and electronics, are permissible when they are used in a permissible way.

### 2. The way of trading must be honest

This second part is often forgotten. A product can be permissible while the sale is not, if the seller lies, hides a fault or cheats on weight, quality or price.

## Principles of honest trade in Islam

Scholars have long taught that a Muslim trader should follow these principles. Details can differ between schools of thought, so for a specific ruling you should ask a qualified scholar.

- **Truthfulness.** Say what the product really is. The traditions of the Prophet (peace be upon him) praise the truthful and trustworthy merchant and warn against deception.
- **Clarity about the price.** The price and what you receive should be clear to both sides, with no hidden charges.
- **No hidden faults.** If a product has a defect, the seller should tell the buyer. Islamic law recognises the buyer's right when a hidden defect is found.
- **No false pressure.** Pretending that stock is running out, or using fake offers to hurry a customer, goes against honest dealing.
- **Delivering what was promised.** Colour, size, material and quality should match the description and photos.
- **Fair profit.** Earning a profit is allowed. Cheating and exploiting someone's need is not.

## Common problems in online shopping

Online trade makes some of these principles harder to follow, which is why they matter more:

- Photos taken from other websites that do not show the real item.
- Descriptions that exaggerate quality or the brand.
- Prices that change after the buyer agrees.
- Items that arrive different from what was shown.

A halal-minded seller avoids all of these on purpose.

## How buyers can shop halal

1. **Check the product.** Make sure it is permissible and suits your need.
2. **Ask for clear details.** Request size, material, colour, price and delivery in writing.
3. **Avoid doubtful sellers.** If a seller is vague or dishonest, choose another.
4. **Keep your own honesty.** Do not ask for a refund by lying, and do not leave false complaints.
5. **Pay what was agreed.** Keep your side of the deal.

## How sellers can trade halal

1. Use real photos of your real stock.
2. Describe products honestly, including small flaws.
3. State the full price and delivery charges before the buyer commits.
4. Reply truthfully about stock and delivery time.
5. Help the buyer if a problem happens, as fairly as you can.

## A note on knowledge

This article is a general guide and not a religious ruling (fatwa). If you have a specific question about a type of sale, such as instalments, advance payment or business partnerships, ask a trusted scholar.
