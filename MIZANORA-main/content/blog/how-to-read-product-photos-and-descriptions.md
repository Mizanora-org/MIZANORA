When you shop online, the photos and the description are all you have. Learning to read them well prevents most disappointments.

## Photos: what to look for

- **Real photos or copied images?** Photos with a clean white background are common, but a shop with only stock photos shows little about the real item. Look for several angles, close-ups and close photos of the material.
- **Colour.** Screens show colours differently. If the exact colour matters, ask support for a photo in daylight.
- **Scale.** Look for a photo next to a hand, a model or a ruler.

## Description: what must be there

A good description states:

1. Material or type.
2. Size or dimensions.
3. Colour options.
4. What is included.
5. Any care or use instructions.

If a description is only one line of marketing words, ask questions before ordering.

## Words that deserve a question

Words such as "premium", "original" or "high quality" mean nothing without details. Ask what the material is and whether there is a brand.

## Check the details around the product

Look at the price, delivery cost, returns page and contact details. A good product page links to a [returns page](/returns) and a way to ask questions. See [how to spot fake online shops](/blog/how-to-spot-fake-online-shops-pakistan).

## Ask the seller smart questions

- What is the material?
- What are the exact measurements?
- Is this the same item as in the photo?
- How many days is delivery to my city?
- Can I exchange the size?

A seller who answers clearly is usually a good sign.

## Photo and description checklist

1. At least three real photos.
2. Material and size stated.
3. Colour checked.
4. Return and exchange rules read.
5. Questions answered before ordering.
