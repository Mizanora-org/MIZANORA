Everyone wants a low price, but the cheapest product is not always the best deal. A fair price is one where quality, delivery and service match what you pay.

## Three questions before you buy

1. **What am I really getting?** Material, size, pieces and brand
2. **What is the total cost?** Product price plus delivery
3. **What happens if it is wrong?** Return and exchange rules

## Signs a price is too low

- Much cheaper than every other seller for the same product
- No clear photos, only a stock picture
- No details about material or size
- The shop pushes you to pay in advance

Read [how to spot fake online shops](/blog/how-to-spot-fake-online-shops-pakistan).

## Signs a price is fair

- Similar to the market rate for the same product
- Several real photos and a clear description
- Cash on delivery and a visible return policy

See [what market rate means](/blog/what-is-market-rate-and-how-to-know-a-fair-price).

## Cost per use

A shirt that costs Rs 1,500 and lasts two winters is cheaper than a Rs 700 shirt that loses shape in a month. Think about how often you will use it.

## When cheap is fine

For things you use rarely or for a short time (a party accessory, a small gadget), a lower price is fine. For shoes, jackets, bags and kitchen tools that you use daily, choose quality.
