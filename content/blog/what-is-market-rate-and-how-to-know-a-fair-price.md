When you see a price with a higher price struck through next to it, the higher number is meant to be the market rate: what the same product usually costs elsewhere. Understanding it helps you judge whether a deal is real.

## What market rate means

Market rate is the common selling price of the same product in other shops or markets. If a shop sells below it, you get a discount. If a shop sells at or above it, there is no discount, and the shop is charging the normal price or more.

## How to check the market rate yourself

1. Search the exact product name in two or three places.
2. Compare the same size, colour, material and pieces.
3. Note the delivery fee in each case.
4. Ignore the lowest and the highest outlier.

A product with a different fabric or fewer pieces is not the same product.

## Why a higher struck price can be misleading

A struck price is only meaningful if it is a real price that shoppers actually paid. If the "old price" was never used, the discount is only a display. Look at the product over a few days. Our guide on [comparing prices before buying](/blog/how-to-compare-prices-before-buying-online) explains this in more detail.

## When no discount is shown

Sometimes a seller sets a price equal to or lower than the market rate for their own comparison. In that case an honest listing shows only the price, without a fake discount.

## Why quality matters more than a small difference

A tiny price difference does not matter if one product is poorly made. Look at material, photos and reviews as well. See [how to check product quality before you order](/blog/how-to-check-product-quality-before-you-order-online).

## Summary

- Market rate = the usual price elsewhere
- Compare the same product, not a similar one
- A discount is real only if the old price was real
- Check the total cost with delivery
