There is a lot of confident talk about Pakistani e-commerce going digital. Very little of it is backed by order-level data. In mid-2026 a first-party study of roughly **170,000 orders** placed over twelve months (June 2025 – May 2026) was published, and it is the clearest picture we have of how Pakistan actually pays ([OrderNation, Report No. 1, June 2026](https://www.ordernation.com/blogs/research/state-of-online-shopping-pakistan-2026)).

Here are the numbers, and what they mean if you shop or sell here.

## The headline figures

| Finding | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Orders placed cash on delivery | **94.5%** |
| Digitally prepaid (card, wallet, bank transfer) | **1.6%** |
| Cancellation rate on COD orders | **37.3%** |
| Cancellation rate on prepaid orders | **2.8%** |
| COD orders cancelled vs prepaid | **more than 13×** |
| Shipped parcels returned to origin | **about 1 in 5** |

Two of those deserve attention immediately.

## 37.3% of cash-on-delivery orders never complete

This is the most under-discussed number in Pakistani e-commerce. A COD order is more than thirteen times more likely to be cancelled than a prepaid one.

The causes are not mysterious:

- **Fake and mistyped orders.** No money has moved, so there is no friction in placing one.
- **Genuine change of mind.** Delivery takes three to nine days, which is long enough to change your mind about almost anything.
- **Confirmation calls that go unanswered.** Shops call to verify; an unanswered call is often cancelled.
- **Refusal at the door**, which is the customer's right and the shop's loss.

And it costs real money: a refused parcel means the shop pays delivery both ways, plus handling, on an item it still has to resell.

## Why this matters to you as a shopper

Two practical consequences, both in your favour and both worth knowing:

**1. Understand why shops call you.** That verification call after a COD order is not spam — it exists because more than a third of COD orders never complete. Answering it is the single easiest way to get your parcel faster.

**2. Do not refuse casually.** Refusing a parcel you ordered is sometimes completely correct — the item is wrong, damaged, or late. But casual refusal is priced into everyone's delivery fees, and it makes shops more reluctant to serve some areas. Order what you intend to keep, and use [the parcel checklist](/blog/parcel-arrived-checklist-what-to-check) rather than a blanket refusal.

## Why this matters if you sell

If you run a small shop, the dataset says three things directly:

- **COD is not a preference, it is the market.** 94.5% is not a segment to upsell; it is the whole business. Any plan that assumes digital payment will lead within a year is not a plan.
- **Your biggest cost is not marketing, it is RTO.** With roughly one in five shipped parcels coming back, reducing returns beats increasing traffic. Better photos, accurate sizes and a confirmation call are cheaper than ads.
- **Verification is worth the awkwardness.** An awkward WhatsApp confirmation is far cheaper than a returned parcel.

The wider policy context agrees: Pakistan's draft National E-Commerce Policy 2.0 (2025–2030) still describes cash on delivery as the default payment method, accounting for roughly 60–70% of e-commerce transactions ([industry summary, 2026](https://www.tycorun.com/blogs/industry-insights/top-10-e-commerce-companies-in-pakistan)).

## Where the digital side genuinely is growing

It would be wrong to read 1.6% as "nothing is changing". The digital rails are being built underneath:

- Digital payment transaction volumes in Pakistan grew **77% year-on-year to PKR 124.7 trillion** in FY2023, according to the State Bank of Pakistan ([SBP figures, reported 2026](https://www.grand-review.com/article/pakistans-e-commerce-boom-2026-digital-payments-online-marketplaces-and-the-shop-mtnepoce)).
- Mobile wallets — JazzCash, Easypaisa, NayaPay, SadaPay — are real alternatives in cities now.
- Last-mile logistics improved sharply, with providers releasing a share of COD value to sellers at pickup instead of after 7–15 days.

But overall volume and e-commerce share are different things. A country can have enormous digital payment growth and still place 94.5% of its online orders in cash, because the two numbers measure different populations.
