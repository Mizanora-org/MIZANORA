Shortage years are when shoppers get hurt, because the tricks get better. A fake capacity label on a power bank is always dishonest; in a shortage year it is also much more likely. Here is what is actually scarce in 2026 and how to buy around it.

## What is genuinely short

Three things, and they are not the ones in the headlines:

**1. Memory.** High-bandwidth memory now takes **23% of total DRAM wafer capacity**, up from single digits two years ago, with up to **70% of all memory chips produced in 2026** expected to go to AI data centres ([Accuris, 2026](https://accuristech.com/blog/ai-data-center-electronic-component-supply/)). The makers — Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron — have preallocated much of their 2026 capacity.

**2. Power management components.** Voltage regulators, converters and gate drivers built on mature process nodes are expected to stay short through 2026. Investment went to advanced nodes for AI; the boring components that every charger needs did not get the same money.

**3. Copper and transformers.** Each megawatt of data centre capacity needs about **27 tons of copper**, and copper hit a record **$6 per pound** in January 2026. Transformer lead times run to years.

Notice what is *not* on the list: nothing about phones themselves being impossible to make. It is the components behind them that are tight, which means prices and specifications move, not availability.

## What that does to what you buy

| Category | Why it moves | How much to worry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power banks | Cells + power ICs | High — also where capacity fraud is worst |
| Phones (top storage tiers) | Memory is the expensive part | High on the top tier, low on the base model |
| Chargers and adapters | Power ICs | Medium |
| Laptops and SSDs | Memory | High |
| Cables | Copper | Low but real |
| Clothing, home, kitchenware | Nothing scarce | None |

That last row is the whole strategy. In a shortage year, **the best value in the shop is often the thing with no chip in it.** A good winter jacket is unaffected by data centre construction.

## Four tricks that get worse in a shortage

1. **Fake capacity.** A power bank labelled 20,000 mAh that weighs almost nothing. Real capacity has weight; if it feels like an empty box, it is close to one.
2. **Refurbished sold as new.** Check the seals, the serial and the battery health before the rider leaves — the routine is in [how to buy electronics safely](/blog/how-to-buy-electronics-online-safely-pakistan).
3. **"Last stock" urgency.** A real shortage is a reason to compare carefully, not a reason to skip comparing.
4. **Specification substitution.** The same model name with a slower chip or less memory inside. Read the specification, not the name: [how to read product photos and descriptions](/blog/how-to-read-product-photos-and-descriptions).

## How to buy well anyway

- **Buy the tier you will use.** The premium for the largest storage option is exactly where scarcity is priced in.
- **Do not wait for a crash.** Analysts expect 30–50% of planned 2026 data centre capacity to slip to 2028, which means demand on the grid and on components stays high for a while ([Omdia, April 2026](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/opinion-omdia-ai-semiconductor-chip-scarcity/817172/)).
- **Keep the box and the bill.** Resale value rises in a shortage year, and you cannot resell without proof.
- **Repair before replacing.** A new battery is a fraction of a new phone.
- **Compare totals, never stickers** — the method is in [how to compare prices](/blog/how-to-compare-prices-before-buying-online).

## The honest bit

Nobody can tell you when this eases. Efficiency gains are real and fast, and so is demand. What you can control is which categories you expose yourself to, and how carefully you check what arrives.

Which is the same discipline that gets you through every other kind of tight market: know the real specification, check the parcel at the door, and use [cash on delivery](/blog/cash-on-delivery-vs-advance-payment-pakistan) so the money moves after the box is open.
