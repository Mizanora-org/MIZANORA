Here is the uncomfortable version of the AI shopping story for small sellers: an agent does not browse. It compares structured facts and picks. If your shop's facts are missing, vague or contradicted by your own photos, you are not ranked lower — you are **not considered at all**.

The good news is that everything an agent needs is also what a hesitant human needs, and none of it costs money.

## Why clarity became the ranking factor

In traditional search, a shop competed on persuasion: a good headline, a nice photo, a persuasive description. In agent-led selection, the agent needs to compare delivery windows, fees and return terms quickly. Ambiguity is expensive for it, so it moves on ([nShift, 2026](https://nshift.com/blog/agentic-commerce-ai-shopping-agents-2026)).

Research puts the same point from the merchant side: brands whose product data is fragmented or incomplete are already losing visibility in agentic searches, and data readiness is now a commercial priority rather than a technical one ([Braze, 2026](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/agentic-commerce)).

Read that again — **data readiness is a commercial priority**. It sits with whoever owns the shop, not with whoever owns the server.

## The zero-budget checklist

Work through this in order. Each item helps human customers today, which is how you know it is worth doing.

### 1. Make the four money facts explicit

Put these where both a person and a machine can read them, on every product:

- **Price** (the real one, with no invented comparison price)
- **Delivery fee** (and whether it changes by city or by cart size)
- **Delivery window** ("2–4 working days", not "fast delivery")
- **Return window** and who pays return postage

### 2. Complete the product attributes

Size, material, colour, what is in the box, compatibility. "Premium quality" is not an attribute. "Cotton, 180 GSM" is.

### 3. Take your own photos

Stock photos are the fastest way to become invisible, and they are everywhere: if ten shops show the identical picture, none of them has handled the item. Our guide to [product photography with your phone](/blog/product-photography-tips-with-your-phone) is enough to beat most competitors here.

### 4. Keep stock status honest

Nothing destroys a shop's reliability faster than selling something it does not have. If an item is out, say so.

### 5. Publish a real address, a real number and a real return policy

This is a trust signal for humans and a verification signal for machines. A shop with no address is not a shop.

### 6. Answer the phone — or the WhatsApp

As AI mediates more transactions, the direct moments where trust is built become rarer and more valuable. A human reply is now a competitive advantage, not a cost centre.

## The Pakistani specifics

Two things change the emphasis here.

**Cash on delivery changes the data you must publish.** With roughly 94.5% of Pakistani online orders placed COD and only 1.6% prepaid across ~170,000 orders in 2025–26 ([OrderNation, 2026](https://www.ordernation.com/blogs/research/state-of-online-shopping-pakistan-2026)), the payment protocols that dominate the agentic-commerce conversation simply do not apply yet. What transfers perfectly well is the *data* layer — price, delivery, returns.

**Cancellation rates make accuracy urgent.** The same dataset found COD orders cancelled at 37.3% versus 2.8% for prepaid. Most of that gap is expectation mismatch: the item was not what the buyer pictured. Accurate photos and attributes are the cheapest lever you have on your single biggest cost.

## What not to do

- **Do not invent structured data.** Marking up facts that are not true is worse than having no markup — for search engines and for customers.
- **Do not chase one protocol.** Several compete and the standards are still moving. Being *legible* is durable; betting on one standard is not. See [the protocol explainer](/blog/ai-agent-payment-protocols-acp-ap2-ucp-explained).
- **Do not buy links or reviews.** Penalties are real, and they are the one mistake in this list that can end a shop.
- **Do not hide problems behind a chatbot.** If something is wrong, a person should say so.

## The SEO half still matters

None of this replaces the basics. Free structured data, a clean sitemap, original titles and real internal links are what let any crawler — human or machine — find you in the first place. > An agent cannot be persuaded. It can only be informed. Publish the truth in a form a machine can read, and you are visible to both.
