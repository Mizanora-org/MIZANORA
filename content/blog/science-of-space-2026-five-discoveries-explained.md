Space news is usually either impenetrable or overhyped. 2026 produced five results that are neither — they are specific, checkable, and genuinely strange. Here they are in plain language.

## 1. Sugar in interstellar space

In July, astronomers reported detecting **erythrulose**, a four-carbon sugar, in a molecular cloud near the centre of our galaxy ([2026 in science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_in_science)). It is described as the first detection of a *true* sugar in the interstellar medium — earlier finds were sugar-*related* molecules like glycolaldehyde.

**Why it matters:** the chemistry that leads to biology does not wait for planets. The ingredients form in the clouds that planets are born from.

## 2. The first confirmed atmosphere on a rocky planet in the habitable zone

Also in July, astronomers confirmed an atmosphere around **LHS 1140 b**, a super-Earth about **49 light-years** away, by detecting helium escaping from its upper atmosphere. It is the first observationally confirmed atmosphere on a rocky exoplanet inside its star's habitable zone.

**Why it matters:** "habitable zone" only means the temperature could allow liquid water. An atmosphere is the next thing you need, and this is the first time we have confirmed one there.

**Why to stay calm:** 49 light-years is about 460 trillion kilometres. Nobody is visiting.

## 3. Planets with the density of candy floss

In June, using NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite, astronomers described two Jupiter-sized "super-puff" planets with densities of **0.038 and 0.047 g/cm³** — among the lowest-density giant planets ever found, and the largest yet discovered below 0.05 g/cm³.

For scale: water is 1 g/cm³. These planets are roughly **25 times less dense than water**. Jupiter is about 1.3 g/cm³.

**Why it matters:** it means our models of how giant planets form and hold onto their atmospheres are still incomplete.

## 4. Eleven thousand new asteroids in one sweep

In April, astronomers using early data from the **Vera C. Rubin Observatory** announced over **11,000 new asteroids**, including hundreds of trans-Neptunian objects and **33 previously unknown near-Earth objects**, confirmed by the Minor Planet Center.

**Why it matters:** this is one observatory, at the start of its survey, finding near-Earth objects at this rate. Cataloguing them is the first step of planetary defence — you cannot track what you have not seen.

## 5. Water ice at the Moon's south pole, and oxygen from lunar soil

Two separate 2026 results point the same way:

- In May, radar data from India's **Chandrayaan-2** orbiter gave strong evidence for subsurface water ice in four permanently shadowed craters near the lunar south pole.
- Blue Origin reported that its "Air Pioneer" reactor extracted oxygen from melted lunar regolith simulant, producing medical- and propellant-grade oxygen.

**Why it matters together:** ice and oxygen are the two things that decide whether people can stay anywhere. Both results say the Moon can supply at least some of both.

## How to read a space story

Four habits that will save you from most bad science news:

1. **Look for the distance.** A discovery 49 light-years away is a discovery, not a destination.
2. **Look for "confirmed".** Detected, candidate and confirmed mean very different things.
3. **Look for the number.** A real report has one — 0.047 g/cm³, 49 light-years, 11,000 asteroids.
4. **Beware "scientists discovered" with no institution.** Real results name the telescope, the mission or the journal.
