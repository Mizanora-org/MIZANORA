Every founder page lists what somebody built. This one is about the part that is harder to see: **why people keep trusting him.** Because a 17-year-old running a marketplace is not trusted for his age — he is trusted for what he does when something goes wrong.

![Muaaz Iqbal, founder and developer of Mizanora, Kasur Punjab Pakistan](/img/muaaz/muaaz-iqbal-02.jpg "Muaaz Iqbal: the character behind Mizanora")

## 1. He says when something is broken

The clearest example is buried in the code. When the GitHub API is unreachable, the live section on his profile page shows a **`CACHED`** badge instead of pretending the numbers are live. When a product's photo stops loading, the product is **hidden** rather than replaced with a stand-in picture.

Both of those cost him something — a smaller catalogue, a less impressive section — and he keeps them anyway.

> A shop that shows a broken image is annoying. A shop that shows a *different* image is dishonest.

That instinct is the whole of [the trust-first theory](/blog/trust-first-theory-why-mizanora-shows-real-prices).

## 2. He answers the phone

There is a WhatsApp number on the shop and a person behind it. In a country where most online sellers vanish the moment a parcel goes wrong, being reachable is not a feature — it is the product.

This is also the reason [cash on delivery](/blog/cash-on-delivery-pakistan) is the default rather than an option. It puts the risk on the shop because the shop is the one that can be asked questions.

## 3. He writes everything down

Every upgrade to Mizanora has a document in the repository (`UPGRADE-v*.md`) and a test file. That is unusual for a solo builder, and it is not bureaucracy — it is how one person stays the conductor of a project across hundreds of sessions.

The same habit shows up as [the small-steps theory](/blog/small-steps-theory-unchi-soch-chhote-qadam): a step small enough to finish and record, every day, in the same direction.

## 4. He never invents a number

No "12,438 people watching". No fake "was Rs 9,999". Every discount refers to a real comparison price, every live badge comes from a real channel state, and every projection in these guides is labelled as a projection.

That restraint is why the numbers that *are* shown can be believed — which is the entire point of publishing [open live stats](/stats) on the shop.

## 5. He builds for the person with the least

The test he uses is blunt: **would this work for a shopper in Kasur, in Urdu, on a cheap phone, on a slow connection?** That single question produced cash on delivery, the Urdu voice assistant, the forgiving search and the zero-Firebase catalogue that keeps the shop alive when free limits run out ([the zero-quota theory](/blog/zero-quota-theory-systems-that-survive-free-limits)).

It also produced [Asaan Lang](/blog/asaan-lang-urdu-programming-language-by-muaaz-iqbal), a programming language in Urdu and Roman Urdu, because the same argument applies to learning: if the barrier is English rather than logic, remove the barrier.

## 6. He is teachable, and he says so

He was born on **27 November 2009** and is studying ICS at Islamia Graduate College. He works with several AI models and is open about which one does what, and about being wrong — see [the AI orchestra theory](/blog/ai-orchestra-theory-claude-gemini-qwen-chatgpt-together).

The combination that makes this work is specific: **big goals, small daily steps, and no pretending.** Most people have one of those three. The one that builds trust in other people is the last.

## The part that is easiest to misunderstand

He is not soft. The balance theory deletes features, hides products and refuses pricing tricks that competitors use daily — see [the Mizan mindset](/blog/muaaz-iqbal-mizan-mindset-balance-theory). Being trustworthy is not the same as being agreeable, and a scale only means something if it sometimes tips against you.
