There is one line in the Mizanora code base that explains more about Muaaz Iqbal than any biography: when a product's photo stops loading, the product is **hidden**. Not replaced with a placeholder. Not shown with a grey box where the picture should be. Hidden, until the picture is fixed.

![Muaaz Iqbal, founder of Mizanora, Kasur Punjab Pakistan](/img/muaaz/muaaz-iqbal-05.jpg "The Trust-First Theory: a shop is only as good as its worst photo")

That single decision costs sales every single day. He keeps it anyway.

## The theory

> Trust is the product. Every other feature is a way of delivering it, and any feature that spends trust to make a sale is a bad trade.

## Why trust comes first in Pakistan specifically

Online shopping in Pakistan has a trust problem that has nothing to do with Mizanora. Shoppers have been sent the wrong item, the wrong size, a picture of something better than what arrived. So the default assumption of a new visitor is not "this shop looks good" — it is "let's see how this one cheats me".

That changes the economics completely. In a market with high trust, a shop optimises for conversion. In a market with low trust, a shop must first *spend* something to earn the right to be believed, and the cheapest currency for that is honest imperfection.

## The rules the theory produces

Once you accept trust-first, a lot of specific decisions stop being debates:

- **Hide, do not fake.** A broken photo is hidden rather than substituted, because a substitute photo is a lie about the product you will receive.
- **Cash on delivery by default.** The customer sees the parcel before the money moves. This is the single biggest trust purchase available to a Pakistani shop, and it is the reason [cash on delivery](/blog/cash-on-delivery-pakistan) is the default rather than an option.
- **Real prices, real discounts.** A discount is only shown when there is a genuine comparison price. No invented "was Rs 9,999". See [how to find real discounts](/blog/how-to-find-real-discounts-online-pakistan).
- **Say when something is cached or offline.** The GitHub section shows a `CACHED` badge when the API is unavailable. Admitting degradation is cheaper than being caught hiding it.
- **Workers set their own price within a band.** Anyone earning from Mizanora plays by visible rules: [how workers set their price](/blog/how-mizanora-workers-set-their-own-price-and-market-rate).

## The cost, named honestly

Trust-first is expensive. Hiding products with broken photos shrinks the catalogue. Cash on delivery means the shop carries delivery risk and RTO losses. Refusing fake discounts makes Mizanora look pricier than a marketplace showing a fake 70% off.

Muaaz's bet is that these costs are one-time and the trust is permanent. A customer who has received exactly what was photographed comes back; a customer who has been fooled once never does, and tells ten people.

That is also the Islamic framing, which is not decoration here. Honest trade is a religious obligation, not a marketing angle — see [halal business and honest trade](/blog/halal-business-and-honest-trade-in-islam-for-online-shops) and [what halal shopping means](/blog/what-is-halal-shopping).

## How to test whether a shop is trust-first

Ask three questions before you order anywhere:

1. Do the product photos look like they were taken in a real room, or are they all identical stock images?
2. Is there a real address, a real WhatsApp number and a real person behind them?
3. Does the shop admit anything? A shop that claims every product is perfect is telling you about itself.

Our own version of that checklist is in [how to spot fake online shops](/blog/how-to-spot-fake-online-shops-pakistan), and it applies to us too.

## The uncomfortable conclusion

Trust-first means you will sometimes look smaller than a competitor who is willing to fake a photo. That is the whole price. Muaaz has decided, repeatedly, that it is worth paying — which is what [the Mizan mindset](/blog/muaaz-iqbal-mizan-mindset-balance-theory) looks like when it costs money.
