There is a building somewhere with 100 to 500 megawatts of equipment inside it — as much power as a city. It is full of computers training AI models. And it is one reason the phone you are thinking of buying costs more than it did two years ago.

Here is the chain, with the numbers.

## The electricity

The International Energy Agency estimates data centres consumed roughly **460 TWh** of electricity globally in 2025, with a base-case projection of **945 TWh by 2030** — more than the entire current electricity demand of Japan ([IEA figures, reported in Semiconductor Engineering](https://electronics-insider.com/blog/ai-energy-efficiency-semiconductor-imperative-2030)). Global data centre electricity demand grew **17% in 2025**, and AI-focused facilities surged **50% in the same year**.

The physical limits are already visible. Gas turbines — the fastest route to new power capacity — are reported booked through 2028. In the US, grid interconnection queues have passed **2,100 gigawatts**, exceeding total grid capacity, and analysts expect **30–50% of planned 2026 data centre capacity to slip to 2028** ([Omdia analysis, April 2026](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/opinion-omdia-ai-semiconductor-chip-scarcity/817172/)).

## The money

The five largest hyperscalers — Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta and Oracle — are projected to spend over **$600 billion** on infrastructure in 2026, about 36% more than 2025, with roughly 75% of it aimed at AI ([Accuris, 2026](https://accuristech.com/blog/ai-data-center-electronic-component-supply/)).

## The chips — and this is the part that reaches you

Two numbers explain most of it:

- Up to **70% of all memory chips produced globally in 2026** are expected to be consumed by AI data centres.
- High-bandwidth memory (HBM) now takes **23% of total DRAM wafer capacity**, up from single digits two years ago.

When the three companies that make most of the world's memory reallocate their cleanroom capacity to higher-margin AI parts, there is less capacity left for the ordinary memory in phones, laptops and power banks. Less supply, same demand, higher price.

Materials moved too: each megawatt of data centre capacity needs about **27 tons of copper**, and copper hit a record **$6 per pound** in January 2026.

## So: why does your phone cost more?

| Step | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | AI data centres bid for memory and power components |
| 2 | Makers shift factory capacity to the higher-margin parts |
| 3 | Consumer-grade memory and power chips get scarcer |
| 4 | Phone, laptop and accessory makers pay more |
| 5 | The price reaches a shop in Kasur |

The practical consequence for a Pakistani shopper: **categories built on memory and power chips move first and fall last.** Power banks, phones, chargers, SSDs and anything with a battery or a chip. Clothing and home goods are largely unaffected — there is no AI data centre bidding for cotton.

That split is the single most useful piece of shopping advice in an AI-boom year, and it is the same logic as [the world economy and your basket](/blog/world-economy-2026-what-it-means-for-pakistani-shoppers).

## What to do with this

1. **Buy memory-heavy items when you see a genuine price, not "later".** Waiting does not help in a shortage year.
2. **Do not over-buy specifications you will not use.** The premium for the top storage tier is where the shortage shows most — see [cheap vs quality](/blog/cheap-vs-quality-how-to-judge-price-pakistan).
3. **Keep devices longer.** The cheapest component is the one you already own.
4. **Treat a suspiciously cheap power bank as a warning.** Capacity fraud is the classic shortage-year trick: [how to buy electronics safely](/blog/how-to-buy-electronics-online-safely-pakistan).

## The counter-trend worth knowing

Efficiency is improving fast. AMD reported an estimated **fourfold increase in AI energy efficiency by mid-2026** against a 2024 baseline, ahead of its own schedule, and it is working towards a 20-fold rack-scale improvement by 2030 ([AMD, August 2026](https://datacenter.news/story/amd-says-ai-energy-efficiency-rises-fourfold-by-2026)) — though those figures combine measured product data with modelled estimates.

So the honest summary is two directions at once: **AI compute is growing faster than the grid, while getting materially more efficient per unit of work.** Which one wins decides whether this reaches your wallet for two years or ten.
