Almost every bad online shopping experience in Pakistan comes from the same handful of mistakes. None of them require expertise to avoid — they require thirty seconds of attention in the right place. Here they are, in the order they cost you money.

## 1. Ordering without checking the size chart

The single most common reason for a return. "Medium" means something different in every factory in the world, and a size chart is the only thing that is actually about *this* item.

**Fix:** measure one piece of clothing you already own and love, then compare it with the chart. Our full method is in [how to choose the right size online](/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-size-online).

## 2. Believing a discount with no reference price

"70% off" is meaningless without knowing what it is 70% off from. Many shops raise the comparison price before the sale.

**Fix:** compare against two other shops for the same item, not against the struck-through number. See [how to find real discounts](/blog/how-to-find-real-discounts-online-pakistan).

## 3. Paying in advance to a seller you have never used

Advance payment to an unknown seller is the most expensive mistake on this list, because there is usually no way back.

**Fix:** use [cash on delivery](/blog/cash-on-delivery-pakistan) for any first order. Pay in advance only after a shop has delivered to you correctly at least once.

## 4. Not reading the photo properly

Photos tell you more than descriptions do, if you know what to look at: background consistency, whether the same item appears from several angles, whether the picture looks like a warehouse or like a magazine.

**Fix:** spend a minute with [how to read product photos and descriptions](/blog/how-to-read-product-photos-and-descriptions).

## 5. Ignoring the delivery charge until checkout

A Rs 1,200 item with Rs 400 delivery is not cheaper than a Rs 1,500 item with free delivery.

**Fix:** always compare the **total** at the door, not the sticker price. The same idea is in [how to compare prices before buying online](/blog/how-to-compare-prices-before-buying-online).

## 6. Buying from a shop with no real address

No address, no WhatsApp number, no person: no recourse.

**Fix:** run the checklist in [how to spot fake online shops](/blog/how-to-spot-fake-online-shops-pakistan).

## 7. Ordering the cheapest version of something you use daily

Cheap is different from good value. A Rs 800 shoe that lasts two months costs more per year than a Rs 2,500 pair that lasts two years.

**Fix:** divide the price by how long you expect it to last. Detailed in [cheap vs quality](/blog/cheap-vs-quality-how-to-judge-price-pakistan).

## 8. Not opening the parcel before the rider leaves

Once the rider has gone, a dispute becomes your word against theirs.

**Fix:** use the [parcel arrival checklist](/blog/parcel-arrived-checklist-what-to-check) while the rider is still there.

## 9. Sharing your CNIC number when it is not needed

No legitimate cash-on-delivery order requires a CNIC number.

**Fix:** read [online shopping privacy](/blog/online-shopping-privacy-protect-your-phone-number-and-address) before you fill in any form.

## 10. Shopping on a lookalike site

Fake shops copy real ones almost perfectly, except for the domain.

**Fix:** check the address bar letter by letter before paying. Mizanora is **mizanora.vercel.app** — anything else is not us.

## 11. Never tracking the order

If you do not track it, you find out a parcel is lost two weeks later instead of two days later.

**Fix:** track it, and read [how to track your order](/blog/how-to-track-your-order-and-read-your-bill).

## The one habit that prevents most of them

Slow down for ninety seconds before pressing order: check the size, the total, the address bar and the payment method. That is it. Everything else on this list is a variation on being in a hurry.
