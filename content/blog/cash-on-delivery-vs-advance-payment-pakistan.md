Ask ten Pakistani shoppers which payment method is better and nine will say cash on delivery. They are mostly right — but "mostly" is doing a lot of work in that sentence, and knowing where COD is weaker will save you money and refused parcels.

## What each method actually protects you against

| | Cash on delivery | Advance payment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Never receive the item | Your money is safe | You must chase a refund |
| Receive the wrong item | You can refuse the parcel | You must arrange a return |
| Receive a damaged item | You can inspect before paying | You must prove it arrived damaged |
| Seller disappears | You lost nothing | You lost everything |
| Cost | Usually higher delivery, sometimes a COD fee | Often cheaper, sometimes free delivery |
| Availability | Not every courier serves every area | Always available |

## The hidden cost of COD nobody mentions

Cash on delivery is not free. Someone has to carry the risk, and that someone prices it in:

- **Higher delivery charges.** Couriers charge more for COD because they handle cash.
- **RTO (return to origin) losses.** When a parcel is refused, the shop pays both ways. This is why shops with heavy RTO start calling customers repeatedly or refusing to serve some areas.
- **Slower dispatch.** Some shops hold COD orders longer to verify them by phone.

None of this means you should stop using COD. It means you should not refuse a parcel casually, because repeated refusals make the whole system worse for the next customer in your area — including you.

## The rule we recommend

> Use cash on delivery for any **first** order from any seller. Switch to advance payment only after that seller has delivered to you correctly at least once, and only for small amounts at first.

That single rule captures almost all of the safety benefit and almost none of the risk. It is the same logic behind [how to spot fake online shops](/blog/how-to-spot-fake-online-shops-pakistan): trust is earned per seller, not per website.

## When advance payment is genuinely fine

- The seller has delivered to you correctly before.
- It is a small amount you would not chase anyway.
- You are using a method with a real dispute process, not a direct bank transfer to a personal account.
- The shop gives a written return policy that names who pays return postage.

## When you should never pay in advance

- The seller exists only as a social-media account with no website and no address.
- You are being pushed: "price goes up in ten minutes" is a pressure tactic, not a reason.
- Payment is requested to a **personal** account in somebody's own name for a supposedly registered business.
- The price is far below every other seller for the same item. That gap is the product.

## What to do if you already paid and the item never came

1. Message the seller once, in writing, with the order details and a clear deadline.
2. If there is no reply, leave a public review on the platform you found them on — this works more often than people expect.
3. If you paid by bank transfer, contact your bank the same day; reversal windows are short.
4. Keep every screenshot. Documentation is the only leverage you have left.
