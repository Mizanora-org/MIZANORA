Ask a Pakistani shopper what they dislike most about ordering online and "delivery charges" is always near the top. Ask what decides that number and almost nobody says the right thing: **it is mostly the price of diesel.**

## The chain, link by link

A parcel from a warehouse in one city to a doorstep in another touches fuel four times:

1. **Line-haul** — the truck that moves sorted parcels between cities. This is the long, expensive leg.
2. **Last mile** — the motorcycle or van that brings it to your door, often after several failed attempts.
3. **Reverse logistics** — the return trip when a parcel is refused. This one is pure loss.
4. **Backup power** — generators at sorting centres and warehouses.

When diesel rises, all four move together. A delivery charge is not a shop being greedy; it is a shop passing on the one cost it cannot avoid.

## Why the charge does not fall as fast as it rises

Shoppers notice this and reasonably suspect foul play. There are two real reasons:

- **Contracts lag.** Courier rates are renegotiated on cycles, not daily, so a fall in oil takes weeks to reach a checkout page.
- **Returns cost more when volumes grow.** Roughly one in five shipped parcels in Pakistan comes back, and every return is a full round trip paid for by somebody. See the data in [what 170,000 Pakistani orders show](/blog/pakistan-cash-on-delivery-2026-what-the-data-shows).

The second point is the one within Pakistan's own control, and it is why the shops that care about your delivery charge are the ones working hardest on accurate photos and sizes.

## What a fair delivery charge looks like in late 2026

There is no official number, but there is a sensible test:

| Test | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Is it stated before checkout? | A charge revealed at the last step is a dark pattern |
| Is it flat, or does it jump by city? | Flat pricing means the shop absorbs the long routes |
| Is there a free-delivery threshold? | If so, is it reachable for a normal basket? |
| Does it change when you add one more item? | If not, consolidating orders is free money |

The practical rule: **compare the total at your door, never the sticker.** A Rs 1,150 item with Rs 250 delivery costs more than a Rs 1,300 item with free delivery. The arithmetic is spelled out in [how to compare prices](/blog/how-to-compare-prices-before-buying-online) and [search by price](/blog/search-by-price-not-by-name-pakistan-shopping).

## Five ways to pay less delivery this month

1. **Combine orders.** One parcel, one charge. Build a list before you buy instead of ordering four times.
2. **Clear the free-delivery threshold deliberately** — but only with things you were going to buy anyway. Adding Rs 900 of junk to save Rs 250 is not a saving.
3. **Answer the confirmation call.** A parcel that goes out once costs half of one that goes out twice.
4. **Get the address right the first time**, including a landmark. Failed attempts are the most expensive thing in last-mile delivery.
5. **Do not refuse casually.** Refusing is sometimes correct — wrong, damaged or late — but casual refusals push everyone's rates up. The full arrival routine is in [the parcel checklist](/blog/parcel-arrived-checklist-what-to-check).

## The wider picture

Energy prices stayed above what planners assumed through 2026, and European inflation readings named energy as the main driver. That is a global story with a very local ending: **every Pakistani parcel is a small quantity of diesel wearing a cardboard box.**

Which is also why the honest shops talk about delivery charges openly instead of hiding them in the item price. Hiding it makes the number look smaller and the shop look dishonest six weeks later when it has to move.
