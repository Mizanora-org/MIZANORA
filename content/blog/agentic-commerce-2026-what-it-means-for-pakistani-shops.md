The biggest change in online shopping in 2026 is not a new marketplace or a new payment app. It is that **the person doing the shopping is sometimes not a person**. AI agents are beginning to research, compare and, in a growing number of cases, complete purchases on somebody's behalf.

Here is what is actually happening, what the numbers support, and — the part that matters for a small shop in Pakistan — what to do about it.

## What changed in 2026

Shopping assistants stopped being demos. Assistants inside general AI tools moved from answering questions to guiding whole purchases, and retailers began deploying their own on-site agents.

The numbers behind that shift, from published industry research:

- AI and agents drove **20% of all retail sales** and about **$262 billion** in spending during the 2025 holiday season, according to Salesforce ([Entagl, 2026](https://www.entagl.com/blog/agentic-commerce-2026-ai-shopping-agents)).
- Shoppers arriving at retailer sites from AI-powered search converted **nine times more often** than those arriving from social media, per the same Salesforce data.
- Bain projects agents will run **$300–500 billion** of US e-commerce by 2030 — roughly 15–25% of the total.
- A 2026 report found **58% of consumers have replaced traditional search with generative AI tools** for product recommendations ([nShift, 2026](https://nshift.com/blog/agentic-commerce-ai-shopping-agents-2026)).

## The honest caveats, because they matter

It would be easy to over-read those figures, so here is the other half:

- **These are mostly projections, not receipts.** Bain's high case is 25% of US e-commerce; Morgan Stanley's is 20%. They disagree at the edges.
- **Most shoppers still will not let an agent buy unsupervised.** Research cited by Braze expects agentic shopping adoption to jump from 19% to 46% by the end of 2026, but only about **10% are willing to let agents operate without oversight** ([Braze, 2026](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/agentic-commerce)).
- **Trust, not technology, is the near-term ceiling.** Repeat and spec-driven purchases flip first. Considered categories like clothing follow much later.

That last point is the whole story for Pakistan, where trust was already the bottleneck before any agent existed.

## Why this is different for Pakistani shops

Agentic commerce assumes the agent can read your shop. It wants structured, machine-readable facts: exact price, delivery fee, delivery window, return terms, availability.

Now hold that next to how Pakistani e-commerce actually works. First-party data from roughly 170,000 Pakistani orders over twelve months (June 2025 – May 2026) found that **94.5% of orders were cash on delivery and just 1.6% were digitally prepaid** ([OrderNation, 2026](https://www.ordernation.com/blogs/research/state-of-online-shopping-pakistan-2026)).

An agent that needs a digital payment rail in order to complete a purchase cannot complete one here, because the rail is barely used. So the agentic wave will arrive in Pakistan **as research and comparison first, and as checkout much later** — if at all in this decade.

That is not a reason to ignore it. It is a reason to prepare for the part that is coming: being *legible*.

## Four things a small shop should do now

None of this needs a budget. All of it helps human customers today, which is the test of whether it is worth doing.

**1. Write the facts down where a machine can read them.** Exact price, exact delivery fee, delivery window, return window. Ambiguity is what makes an agent skip you — and it is what makes a human hesitate too.

**2. Keep your product data complete.** Incomplete or fragmented product data is already costing shops visibility in AI-led searches ([Braze, 2026](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/agentic-commerce)). A product with no size, no material and no real photo is invisible twice: to people and to agents.

**3. Make delivery promises you can keep.** In agentic commerce, delivery is part of the *selection* decision, not just something that happens after it. A shop that promises two days and takes nine will be filtered out — by an algorithm instead of by a disappointed customer.

**4. Keep the direct relationship.** As agents mediate more transactions, the direct touchpoints where trust is built become rarer and more valuable. A WhatsApp number answered by a real person is worth more in 2026 than it was in 2022, not less.
