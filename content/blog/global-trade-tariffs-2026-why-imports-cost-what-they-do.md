Trade news sounds distant until you notice that the same model of earbuds costs Rs 1,800 in one shop and Rs 3,400 in another. The gap is not mystery — it is a stack of costs, and most shoppers have never seen the stack written down.

## The five layers on every imported item

| Layer | What it is | Roughly what it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Factory gate** | What the manufacturer charges | the base |
| **Freight + insurance** | Container, ship, port, truck | rises with oil and with route risk |
| **Duty and taxes** | Tariffs, customs, sales tax | set by policy, changes with each budget |
| **Currency** | Rupees per dollar at the day of purchase | moves whether or not anything else does |
| **Middlemen** | Importer, distributor, retailer margin | the layer that varies most between shops |

That last row is the interesting one. The first four are the same for everyone. **The number of hands an item passes through is the biggest reason two shops disagree.**

## Why 2026 made this layer more visible

Three things happened at once:

- **Trade routes kept shifting.** Tariff deals and retaliations changed which route made sense, and longer routes mean more freight and more insurance.
- **Currency pressure continued.** A weaker rupee against the dollar raises the cost of every import regardless of what the factory charged. The mechanism is the same one described in [the world economy and your basket](/blog/world-economy-2026-what-it-means-for-pakistani-shoppers).
- **Component shortages raised the factory price itself.** In categories that use memory and power chips, the base cost went up — not the duty, not the freight, the actual chip. That story is in [why gadget prices moved in 2026](/blog/chip-shortage-2026-gadget-prices-pakistan).

## What this means if you are shopping

**Buy local where local is genuinely good.** Clothing, home textiles, kitchenware and many gift items are made here. When the factory is in Punjab, four of the five layers above shrink dramatically. That is the cheapest saving available to a Pakistani shopper and almost nobody frames it that way.

**Be suspicious of a price that is far below everyone else's.** In a market where four of the five layers are identical across shops, a price that is 40% lower is usually telling you about the item, not about the shop's generosity. The full checklist is in [how to spot fake online shops](/blog/how-to-spot-fake-online-shops-pakistan) and [cheap vs quality](/blog/cheap-vs-quality-how-to-judge-price-pakistan).

**Check imports sooner rather than later when the rupee is sliding.** Waiting does not help with imported goods in a falling-currency year. It is the opposite of the advice for seasonal clothing, which really does get cheaper — see [the best time to shop](/blog/best-time-to-shop-online-pakistan-sale-seasons).

**Read the specification, not the adjective.** Two chargers that look identical can have genuinely different components inside, which is exactly what a shortage year produces. Our checklist is in [how to buy electronics safely](/blog/how-to-buy-electronics-online-safely-pakistan).

## Honest uncertainty

Nobody — not a shop, not an analyst — can tell you:

- what the rupee will be worth in three months;
- which tariff changes land in the next budget;
- whether a given shipping route stays open and cheap.

What is knowable is the structure. Once you can see the five layers you stop asking "why is this expensive?" and start asking the useful question: **which layer am I paying for, and is there a version of this item that skips it?**
